Art and media educator

I teach art, art history, media studies, and design, and I treat my classrooms as places to work out what new media environments mean for the oldest questions in teaching: how young people learn to look closely, read carefully, and make deliberately. Attention, in my classes, is a craft to be practiced rather than a resource to be demanded. In my art history course, essays give way to auction catalogue entries, pitch packages, and journal submissions; in the studio, projects begin with a written intention and end in front of an audience. Students write differently when the reader is someone other than the teacher.

The same instincts make me a builder and a researcher. When a lesson needs an instrument that does not exist, I write it myself, and the results are collected at classroomtools.io: small pieces of software for annotating a text together, arranging images into constellations, and seeing where a class looks first. Teaching After the Feed, an action-research project run inside my own classroom, asks what algorithmic culture changes about all of this.
